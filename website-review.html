Notes on your website

I read your public website and wrote down what I noticed. Most of it already works well. These are a few small things that could make it even easier for customers to reach you.

I’m new to the electrical business, and I only looked at the public website. I don’t know your customers, your history, or why things are set up the way they are. Please read these as questions and ideas, not instructions.

What already works well

  • Your phone number and both license numbers are on every page.
  • The services are described in real detail, from generators and panel upgrades to commercial build-outs and fire alarms.
  • The contact page gives your hours, says 24/7 emergency service is available, and promises a follow-up within one business day.
  • Free estimates and your IBEW Local 915 partnership are easy to find.

A few things I noticed

  • The contact form offers “Emergency Service” and “ASAP / Emergency,” but nothing beside those choices says to call instead.
  • The form doesn’t ask for a city or ZIP, so someone may need to call back just to check the area.
  • The homepage says 50+ projects completed; the About page says 500+.
  • Each “Learn more” link on the homepage goes to the same services page.

Checked on the public website November 1 and again November 3, 2026. None of this means you’re losing customers. It’s simply what I noticed.

Small fixes worth confirming

Wording and links

  • “Industry Experiance” on the About page should read “Industry Experience.”
  • “across throughout Florida” on the Services page has one word too many.
  • The Privacy Policy and Terms of Use links at the bottom of each page go back to the homepage instead of their own pages.
  • The site says both “Central Florida” and “Serving All of Florida.” Worth confirming which areas you want to be known for.
  • The headline “Licensed. Insured. Done Right.” builds trust. A short line naming the work could help first-time visitors too.

Before changing any facts

You’d confirm the facts first: project counts, licenses, service areas, and permission to use any photos. I’d keep a note of who approved each change and when.

When a change is done

You’ve approved it, it’s live, and it’s been checked on a phone and a computer, with the links and the contact form still working.

Pages read: sspro.us, sspro.us/about, sspro.us/services, and sspro.us/contact.

The basic customer journey

First: the basics a customer notices right away.

  1. Agree on one short description of what you do, and use it everywhere.
  2. Keep one sheet of approved facts: project counts, license, phone, and hours.
  3. Write down exactly which areas you serve.
  4. Put what you do in the homepage headline.
  5. Keep the menu short, with everyday words.
  6. Give business, low-voltage, and home customers their own starting point.
  7. Make each service link open that service.
  8. Add a city or ZIP box to the contact form.
  9. Make it easy to tap and call from a phone.
  10. Send an agreed test message through the form to make sure it arrives.

Help customers decide

Next: help customers choose you.

  1. Keep the emergency number clearly separate from the estimate form.
  2. Point the Privacy and Terms links to their own pages.
  3. Give each type of work a short overview page.
  4. Write pages for the services you most want to be known for.
  5. Explain how an estimate starts and what happens next.
  6. Use real photos of your work and team, with permission.
  7. Show finished projects, grouped by type of work.
  8. Write short project stories: the need, the work, and the result.
  9. Show where any rating or review comes from.
  10. Introduce the people behind the company.

Keep information consistent

Keep the details the same everywhere.

  1. Word the license consistently on every page, using approved facts.
  2. Add a page listing the areas you serve.
  3. Answer the questions customers ask most.
  4. Proofread every page.
  5. Keep fonts, colors, and buttons consistent.
  6. Check pages on phones and tablets.
  7. Check keyboard access, labels, readability, and contrast.
  8. Give each page a clear title describing its subject.
  9. Confirm the correct public business address, hours, and phone.
  10. Match approved details across listings on other websites.

Test and maintain the work

Keep it working over time.

  1. Make photos load quickly.
  2. When a page moves, point the old link to the new one.
  3. Check how fast pages load on a phone.
  4. Test the menu, buttons, and form before changes go live.
  5. If you’d like, count form messages and phone taps from the site.
  6. Note which requests are the kind of work you want more of.
  7. Compare later numbers with where we started.
  8. Name who keeps each part of the website up to date.

The office kit

Three routines cover customer follow-up, website updates, and a weekly check-in. The kit has a workbook with blank sheets and made-up examples, instructions, and four message drafts you can edit. It doesn’t send anything or connect to your systems. If your current software already does this, we’d use that instead.
