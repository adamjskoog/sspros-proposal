EVALUATION NOTES · NOVEMBER 2026

A useful development test.
A limited claim.

The prototype produced answers to 40 fictional, author-written inquiries on November 1, 2026. Regrading those same answers with revised checks yields 39/40 passing automatic checks. All six labeled urgent cases were routed to a person. The draft-wording scan flagged zero drafts. These results are not a certification of safety or accuracy on company messages.

What changed in the checks

The first grader could pass a wrong timeframe, an unrelated next action, a spelled-out price, and an appointment confirmation. Regression checks now cover those demonstrated misses. Timing is compared with explicit expected patterns; selected content requirements and cross-field consistency are checked. The wording scan recognizes additional price and scheduling phrases.

The revised checks flag C25 because the model said the website form did not include contact details. Only inquiry text was supplied, so that statement about form metadata was unsupported. The answer remains available exactly as recorded. No new model run was needed for this regrading.

What still needs judgment

Pattern matching cannot assess every meaning or implication. During a developer spot review, C19’s notes described an overloaded circuit as though that cause had been established. That is an inference requiring qualified review. C25’s metadata assertion and C19’s technical inference demonstrate why an automatic pass is insufficient.

The review did not establish correct electrical classifications. The current service, coverage, and urgency rules are development assumptions. Qualified company staff would define the appropriate rules and expected answers before a pilot.

A rubric for reviewing an answer

  1. Faithfulness: can every factual statement be traced to the message or an approved company fact?
  2. Useful extraction: are location, scope, timeframe, and genuinely missing details correct?
  3. Next action: is the suggested action appropriate under company policy, with an actual person to review and accept it?
  4. Customer draft: does it ask only useful questions and avoid unsupported technical advice, prices, scheduling, and availability?
  5. Exception handling: are uncertainty, contradictions, and urgent concerns brought to the right person?

Keep development and validation separate

The 40 examples remain development fixtures. A later held-out set should be labeled by qualified staff before prompt changes are tuned against it. Include ambiguous wording, negation, historical hazards, mixed requests, missing reply information, non-English messages, and attempts to change the tool’s instructions. Also check timeouts, unusable output, duplicate requests, and usage limits.

Compare assistance with the existing process and approved reply templates. Measure review time and correction effort, not just generation speed. Repeat important cases to understand variation.

Before a company trial

  • Approve the scope, handling policy, permitted information, accounts, storage, and deletion arrangements.
  • Keep real-message evaluations in private storage outside this public project. Public exports are restricted to an allowlist of fictional inputs and require explicit action.
  • Confirm reviewers, accepted handoffs, a staffed escalation process, a backup person, and the manual fallback.
  • Enforce critical constraints in the application before connecting assignment, reminders, or sending. The prototype currently suggests actions; it does not operate those integrations.
  • Agree acceptance measures from the baseline. A critical failure holds back expansion and triggers review.

Reproducibility

The revised record includes the grading version and hashes of the dataset, grader, and original saved run. It preserves the model, configuration, and generation timestamp from that run. The original run did not record prompt/code hashes, so those cannot be reconstructed as verified generation provenance. New runs record them.
