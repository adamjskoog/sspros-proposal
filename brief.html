Work you can inspect

Using AI tools, I built a sample commercial service page, a tool that sorts customer emails and drafts replies, a review of the current website, eight blank office templates, and this proposal.

The first 90 days

Days 1–30 · Learn and deliver
Finish the first service page, learn how customer requests move through the office, and measure how long it takes today.

Days 31–60 · Own and test
Own website updates and follow-up preparation. Test the AI tool on real company emails, with names and contact details removed, against checks we agree on.

Days 61–90 · Compare and decide
If the test goes well, try it on a small number of new requests, with a person approving every reply. Compare time, quality, and cost, then decide whether to expand, adjust, or stop.

What I would own

  • Keep website wording, facts, and pages current.
  • Keep records of customer requests, draft replies, and follow-up reminders.
  • Test and maintain approved AI tools, with people reviewing the outputs.
  • Write short instructions and provide a weekly progress update.

Tool costs: $400 a month, never more

The company provides and pays for two AI accounts in its own name: Claude Max ($200 tier) and ChatGPT Pro with Codex ($200 tier). No pay-as-you-go credits, add-ons, or usage fees; those settings stay off. If a limit is reached, the work waits or is done by hand. Any change needs written approval.

Website changes, templates, and tools stay in company accounts. If the role ends, I hand everything over with written notes.

How we would judge the work

How quickly customers hear back, whether requests arrive with the details you need, whether every request has someone responsible and a next step, and staff time per email. Any time savings must count the time spent checking and fixing.

The decision to make together

Choose a first assignment, agree paid hours and compensation, name a manager and approver, and set a delivery date and first review. A 30-minute conversation is the next step.
