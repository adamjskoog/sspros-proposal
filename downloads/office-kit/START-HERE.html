SSPros office routines

Prepared for discussion · November 2026

Start with one routine and try it for a week. The first three tabs in SSPros-Office-Routines.xlsx are blank working templates. The Examples tab contains fictional records, kept separate from your work.

Set it up together

  1. Choose one routine and name its owner, backup, and approver.
  2. Agree where the working copy will live and when it will be reviewed. Use the company’s existing system if it already handles this work.
  3. Open the Examples tab, then enter one real item in the matching working tab. Agree its next action and date.
  4. After a week, check whether the routine made the next steps clearer. Remove fields that do not help.

Open and edit

Open the XLSX in Excel, or import it into Google Sheets and check the imported dates and dropdowns before use. Edit the pale input cells. In Follow-ups, the grey Attention column is a formula. It checks for missing fields, overdue dates, and outcomes on closed requests. Dates compare with the current day when the workbook recalculates. Nothing sends a reminder or contacts a customer.

The working tables have 50 rows. Within those rows, filters and dropdowns are ready. To add more, extend the table and copy the preceding row’s formula and validation; check the new row before relying on it. CSV alternatives contain the same input columns, but no formatting, dropdowns, or automatic flags. Dates in CSV examples use YYYY-MM-DD.

Keep a blank master and store the working copy in company-controlled storage. Use record IDs and source links where possible instead of copying customer information. The Examples tab and all files ending in -examples.csv contain fictional data.

What’s included

  • SSPros-Office-Routines.xlsx: three blank working tabs and a separate Examples tab.

Customer follow-up

Know who is waiting and what happens next.

When: At the start of the day and after each customer contact.

Responsible: One named office contact keeps the record current. The estimator or project lead makes scope, price, and scheduling decisions.

  1. Capture the request once. Link to the original message or the record you already use.
  2. Give every open item a person, a specific next action, and an agreed follow-up date. Waiting for a customer still needs a next check-in.
  3. Review overdue and due-today items first. If a date slips, record why and agree the new date instead of silently moving it.
  4. After contact, record what happened and set the next step. Close only when the outcome or accepted handoff is recorded.

Finished when: Every open request has an owner, an action, and a due date. Closed requests have a recorded outcome or a handoff accepted by the next person.

If something stalls: If a request raises an urgent concern, use the company’s agreed escalation process immediately. This sheet does not send alerts or replace that process.

Field guide

  • Reference: use the existing inquiry or job ID so related records can be found. Request: one sentence describing the customer’s need.
  • Owner: a person who has accepted the next action. Next action: a verb and a result, such as ‘Ask for the floor plan’ rather than ‘Follow up’.
  • Due: the next internal action date, not a customer appointment or promised completion date. Enter a real spreadsheet date.
  • Status: New, Waiting for customer, Waiting for team, or Closed. Last update: the latest contact, result, or reason a date changed.
  • Source or outcome: link to the source record while open. On closing, record the result or who accepted the handoff and where the next action is tracked.

Allowed statuses: New; Waiting for customer; Waiting for team; Closed.

Website updates

Keep the wording, approval, and final check together.

When: Whenever a website correction or new page is proposed.

Responsible: The person making the edit owns the task through the final check. A named company approver confirms the facts and wording.

  1. Record the page, the proposed change, and the source needed to confirm it.
  2. Prepare the exact wording or page preview. Record the approver and the dated approval for that version.
  3. After approval, save the previous version and publish using the agreed account and access.
  4. Check the published page on a phone and desktop. Test affected links and record the URL, check date, and any remaining action.

Finished when: The approved version is live, the affected content and links have been checked, and the result is recorded. Approval alone does not finish the task.

If something stalls: If the fact is unconfirmed, keep the change in ‘Needs facts’. If a live check fails, record the problem and restore the previous version or arrange a correction with the site owner.

Field guide

  • Page: the exact page or profile being changed. Proposed change: link to the draft and name its version when wording changes.
  • Fact or source to confirm: the document or person supporting the claim. Leave unknown facts unresolved rather than guessing.
  • Approver and Approval date: the person and date attached to that version’s approval. Blank dates mean approval has not been recorded.
  • Owner and Due: the editor and next internal delivery date. Status: move from Needs facts through review, approval, publishing, and checking.
  • Published check record: the final URL, reviewer, check date, and result. Next action: the step still needed; a ‘Checked’ item has no unresolved correction.

Allowed statuses: Needs facts; Ready for review; Approved; Published - check due; Checked.

Weekly handoff

Bring the owner a short list of decisions.

When: Before the agreed weekly check-in and before someone covers the work.

Responsible: The person maintaining the website and follow-ups prepares the update. The manager resolves priorities and assigns decisions.

  1. Review the existing follow-up and website records. Link to them instead of copying every detail.
  2. List what finished, what is waiting, and the exact decision needed. Include the effect of leaving a decision open.
  3. Agree an owner and date for each next step. Write the decision back into the original record after the meeting.
  4. For time away, name the cover person and confirm they can access the records and understand the next actions.

Finished when: Each open decision has a person and a date. Decisions are written back into the source records, and any cover person has accepted the handoff.

If something stalls: A time-sensitive issue goes to the responsible person when it arises. It does not wait for the weekly meeting.

Field guide

  • Week of: the Monday starting the review week. Type: Finished, Decision needed, Next priority, or Cover handoff.
  • Item or reference: a source record ID or link. Update or decision needed: the result, exact question, or handoff to accept.
  • Owner: the person responsible for the next decision or action. Due: when it is needed; leave blank only for completed items.
  • Status: Open, Decision recorded, Handoff accepted, or Done. Recording a decision also means updating the original task.
  • Keep the update short. Bring unresolved decisions and significant changes rather than every routine contact.

Allowed statuses: Open; Decision recorded; Handoff accepted; Done.

Message drafts

Use a draft only after checking the source message and the company’s preferred wording. Replace every bracketed field, remove questions already answered, and have the agreed reviewer approve the final message. These drafts do not send anything.

Ask for one missing detail

Hi [Name], thanks for the details about [project]. Could you send [specific missing item]? That will help [responsible person] review [the next step].

Example for EX-101: “Thanks for the details about the Tampa office build-out. Could you send the floor plan you mentioned? That will help our estimator review the request.”

After sending: record the contact and choose the next internal check-in date. Do not promise a visit or quote turnaround that has not been agreed.

Follow up on an estimate

Hi [Name], I’m following up on estimate [reference and version] for [scope], sent on [date]. Do you have any questions about the scope or the next step? If your timing or plans have changed, please let us know.

If the customer asks to change the scope, route it to the estimator before sending a new price or confirming work. Record the question and who will answer it.

Request a website approval

Please review [page or draft link, version]. The proposed change is [specific change], based on [confirmed source]. I need your decision on [exact wording or question] by [internal date]. Once approved, I’ll publish it and check the result on a phone and desktop.

After approval: record the approver, date, and approved version. A later wording change needs its own review.

Send a short weekly update

Week of [Monday date]

  • Finished: [result and source record].
  • Decision needed: [question, who can answer, and why it matters].
  • Next: [action, owner, and due date].
  • Cover, if needed: [person, accepted handoff, and where the records are].

After the discussion: put decisions and new dates back into the original records.
