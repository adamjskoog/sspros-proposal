A CLOSER LOOK AT THE OFFICE KIT

Three routines. A clear next step.

Start with whichever job would help most. Each routine includes a blank working sheet, a filled-in example, and instructions for keeping it useful.

These are proposed routines and made-up examples. We’d adapt one to the way your office already works.

01Customer follow-upKnow who is waiting and what happens next.

WHEN TO USE IT

At the start of the day and after each customer contact.

  1. Capture the request once. Link to the original message or the record you already use.
  2. Give every open item a person, a specific next action, and an agreed follow-up date. Waiting for a customer still needs a next check-in.
  3. Review overdue and due-today items first. If a date slips, record why and agree the new date instead of silently moving it.
  4. After contact, record what happened and set the next step. Close only when the outcome or accepted handoff is recorded.

Finished when: Every open request has an owner, an action, and a due date. Closed requests have a recorded outcome or a handoff accepted by the next person.

02Website updatesKeep the wording, approval, and final check together.

WHEN TO USE IT

Whenever a website correction or new page is proposed.

  1. Record the page, the proposed change, and the source needed to confirm it.
  2. Prepare the exact wording or page preview. Record the approver and the dated approval for that version.
  3. After approval, save the previous version and publish using the agreed account and access.
  4. Check the published page on a phone and desktop. Test affected links and record the URL, check date, and any remaining action.

Finished when: The approved version is live, the affected content and links have been checked, and the result is recorded. Approval alone does not finish the task.

03Weekly handoffBring the owner a short list of decisions.

WHEN TO USE IT

Before the agreed weekly check-in and before someone covers the work.

  1. Review the existing follow-up and website records. Link to them instead of copying every detail.
  2. List what finished, what is waiting, and the exact decision needed. Include the effect of leaving a decision open.
  3. Agree an owner and date for each next step. Write the decision back into the original record after the meeting.
  4. For time away, name the cover person and confirm they can access the records and understand the next actions.

Finished when: Each open decision has a person and a date. Decisions are written back into the source records, and any cover person has accepted the handoff.

Days 31–60 · Own & evaluate

Take responsibility for the regular work.

Keep the agreed tasks current while testing whether AI assistance improves the existing process.

WHAT I’D DELIVER
  • Maintain the website and follow-up routine, with a short weekly update.
  • Agree service, urgency, escalation, and data rules before testing company messages.
  • Evaluate approved cases privately, with unnecessary identifiers removed and answers reviewed by qualified staff.
  • Compare total handling time and corrections with the existing process and simple templates.
Review at day 60

Does the evidence support a small supervised trial? Any unresolved critical error holds back expansion.

Days 61–90 · Trial & decide

Expand only where the work holds up.

If the agreed checks pass, try assistance on a small workload with a person reviewing every customer reply.

WHAT I’D DELIVER
  • Record review decisions, corrections, exceptions, and actual time spent.
  • Keep a named backup and the existing human process ready for outages or uncertain cases.
  • Write short operating notes and test that someone else can cover the work.
  • Recommend expanding, adjusting, or stopping; agree the ongoing role and compensation.
Review at day 90

Are the quality and net benefit sufficient? Choose the next responsibility using measured results, not the calendar.
