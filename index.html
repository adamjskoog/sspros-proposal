01 / WHERE I’D START

Two small website fixes.
Quick to check, easy to undo.

I noticed these while reading your public website on November 1. You know your customers far better than I do, so please treat them as questions for you, not corrections.

01

Point emergencies to the phone.

The contact form lets people choose “Emergency Service” or “ASAP / Emergency.” Someone with a real emergency might send the form and then wait for a reply. A short line beside those choices could send them straight to the phone.

Done when: you approve the wording, and it shows correctly on a phone and a computer.

A possible wording

For an emergency, please call (727) 761-7767 now. Don’t wait for a reply to this form.

The form asks for a name, contact details, the type of service, timing, and project details, but not the city or ZIP. One small field could save a phone call just to check whether a job is in your area.

Done when: you agree the field is worth it, and test messages still reach the right inbox.

02 / SORTING WHAT COMES IN

One place for everything that comes in.
A person over every lane.

Calls, emails, texts, and website messages each arrive in their own place. I built an example dashboard to show one way to bring them together: everything lands in one place, emergencies rise to the top, and AI helpers prepare each request for the right person.

  • Emergencies first. A safety check catches words like “sparks” or “burning,” even in Spanish, and sends them straight to a person.
  • AI prepares, people decide. Each request comes with the key details and a draft reply. Your team approves, edits, or reassigns it.
  • Nothing slips. Every request shows who has it and whether it’s waiting on you or on the customer.

03 / WHERE AI COULD HELP

A few places AI could save time.
We’d pick one together.

First I’d ask which software you already use for jobs and customers. Many of those programs already have reminders and AI features built in, and using what you already pay for comes before building anything new.

  1. Partly built and tested

    Sorting what comes in.

    The example dashboard. The website-message part is built and tested: it pulls out the key details, flags emergencies, and drafts a polite reply. Calls, emails, and texts would come next.

    A person still: approves every reply and handles every emergency.

    Open the dashboard
  2. Idea

    Proposals from the estimator’s notes.

    The estimator jots down the scope and notes. AI turns them into a clean, consistent written proposal, ready for review.

    A person still: sets every price and checks every line.

  3. Idea

    Maintenance reminders.

    Generator and EV charger customers get a friendly note when service is due, which can bring back repeat work.

    A person still: chooses who gets reminders and what they say.

  4. Idea

    Job notes from a voice memo.

    Someone on site records a quick voice note. AI turns it into a tidy written note for the job file.

    A person still: checks the note is right before it’s saved.

  5. Idea

    Getting the word out.

    After a finished job, AI drafts a short project story and a Facebook or LinkedIn post from the photos and notes, plus a friendly note asking the customer for a review.

    A person still: approves every post and gets permission for every photo.

  6. Idea to talk about

    Finding public bids.

    Cities, counties, school districts, and the state post electrical, low-voltage, and fire alarm work online. A simple tool could check those sites each day and send a short list of new bids in your area. Where a site offers its own email alerts, we’d use those first and follow each site’s rules.

    A person still: decides which bids are worth going after.

I tested the website-message sorting on 40 made-up messages. It sent every emergency to a person, and it still made mistakes a person would need to catch. Read the testing notes.

05 / COST & HOW WE’D WORK

What it costs.
How we’d work.

Digital Operations & AI Automation

A paid role, starting with the website and customer follow-up. AI help grows only where it proves useful.

AI tools

Between $120 and $400 a month. Your choice.

AI tools would cost $120 to $400 a month, depending on the plans you pick. $400 is the most it would ever be, tax included. Hours and pay are separate. We could start small and move up only if the work calls for it.

  • Starter

    $120 / month

    One tool on its larger plan, such as Claude Max ($100), and the other on its basic plan, such as ChatGPT Plus ($20).

  • Middle

    $200 / month

    Both tools on their $100 plans: Claude Max and ChatGPT Pro.

  • Full

    $400 maximum / month

    Both tools on their top plans: Claude Ultra and ChatGPT Pro ($200 each). If tax would push it past $400, one plan steps down.

A company plan is also an option for either tool, such as Claude Team or ChatGPT Business. The accounts would then belong to the company from day one.

  • Company accounts. The company pays for and owns the accounts, so you see every bill and keep everything if I leave.
  • Never over $400. No add-ons or automatic top-ups. Any change gets your OK in writing first.
  • Automations are separate. These plans are for working with the tools. If we later let something run on its own, like sorting website messages automatically, that needs a small separate account with a spending cap. At your volume it would likely be a few dollars a month, and I’d bring you the cost first.
  • If a tool is down. Customer messages go back to the usual way of handling them.

Prices checked November 3, 2026, before tax, billed monthly: Claude pricing · ChatGPT pricing. Prices can change, so we’d confirm them before buying.
