# Message drafts

Use a draft only after checking the source message and the company’s preferred wording. Replace every bracketed field, remove questions already answered, and have the agreed reviewer approve the final message. These drafts do not send anything.

## Ask for one missing detail

Hi [Name], thanks for the details about [project]. Could you send [specific missing item]? That will help [responsible person] review [the next step].

Example for EX-101: “Thanks for the details about the Tampa office build-out. Could you send the floor plan you mentioned? That will help our estimator review the request.”

After sending: record the contact and choose the next internal check-in date. Do not promise a visit or quote turnaround that has not been agreed.

## Follow up on an estimate

Hi [Name], I’m following up on estimate [reference and version] for [scope], sent on [date]. Do you have any questions about the scope or the next step? If your timing or plans have changed, please let us know.

If the customer asks to change the scope, route it to the estimator before sending a new price or confirming work. Record the question and who will answer it.

## Request a website approval

Please review [page or draft link, version]. The proposed change is [specific change], based on [confirmed source]. I need your decision on [exact wording or question] by [internal date]. Once approved, I’ll publish it and check the result on a phone and desktop.

After approval: record the approver, date, and approved version. A later wording change needs its own review.

## Send a short weekly update

Week of [Monday date]

- Finished: [result and source record].
- Decision needed: [question, who can answer, and why it matters].
- Next: [action, owner, and due date].
- Cover, if needed: [person, accepted handoff, and where the records are].

After the discussion: put decisions and new dates back into the original records.
