01

Start with the workflow.

Use the team’s existing tools. Agree what a useful handoff contains before adding automation.

02

Use AI where it earns its place.

A later pilot could summarize requests and draft replies from approved information, with a person reviewing each one.

03

Measure it before expanding.

Compare handling time and correction rates with the manual process. Keep pricing, scheduling, and technical decisions with the team.

What would make an AI pilot ready for real use?

A bounded job: extract service, location, timing, and missing details into a consistent record. Treat customer text as information, never as instructions to the system.

A small evaluation set: review at least 30 approved, redacted examples, including incomplete, ambiguous, and urgent requests. Check extraction accuracy, unsupported statements, and appropriate escalation before a limited pilot.

An accountable release: agree the data policy and spending limit, record model and prompt versions, log corrections, require human approval before sending, and retain a manual fallback. Continue only when the team sees useful, repeatable results.

This is a proposed implementation approach. The prototype above does not perform AI inference, send messages, store inquiries, or integrate with business systems.

04 / THE FIRST 90 DAYS

A focused start.
More responsibility as it works.

Agree the hours, priorities, and access first. Review completed work at each checkpoint and adjust the next phase together.

Days 1–30 · Learn & deliver

Get the foundations right.

Learn how inquiries are handled today. Agree the most useful first assignments and establish a simple baseline.

WHAT I WOULD DELIVER
  • An approved public facts sheet and ranked correction list.
  • Revised homepage copy and two priority service-page drafts.
  • A map of the inquiry process, with ownership and handoffs.
  • A baseline for response time, missing details, and handling effort.
Review at day 30

Are the drafts accurate and useful? Have we identified one workflow worth improving?

Days 31–60 · Own & improve

Own the recurring work.

Take responsibility for two agreed routines. Test a small improvement alongside the existing process.

WHAT I WOULD DELIVER
  • Two maintained routines: public-content upkeep and inquiry follow-up support, if agreed.
  • Three adapted templates and an inquiry-form specification.
  • One approved project story, if publishable material is available.
  • A limited handoff pilot, with human review and recorded corrections.
Review at day 60

Are requests easier to hand off? Does the pilot save effort without increasing corrections?

Days 61–90 · Measure & expand

Show the results. Choose what’s next.

Make the work maintainable and review its value with the people who use it.

WHAT I WOULD DELIVER
  • A before-and-after review using the agreed measures.
  • Short operating instructions and a clear backup owner.
  • A maintained decision log and prioritized next-quarter backlog.
  • A recommendation to continue, adjust, or stop each pilot.
Review at day 90

Which responsibilities should become permanent? Agree the ongoing role, scope, and compensation.

Four measures worth watching.

Response time

Median time from receipt to first human response.

Handoff completeness

Share of requests with agreed essential details.

Follow-up coverage

Open requests with an owner and dated next action.

Handling effort

Minutes per request, including review and corrections.

Targets would be agreed after establishing the baseline. Delivery depends on the agreed schedule, access, approvals, and available project material.
