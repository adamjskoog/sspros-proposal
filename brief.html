Digital Operations & AI Automation

I’d like to join your team in a paid role looking after the website, customer follow-up, and simple AI tools that save time.

I’m new to the electrical business, and I won’t pretend otherwise. I’d start by learning how you already work, then help wherever you tell me it’s useful. You’d approve anything a customer sees.

Where I’d start

Two small website fixes, quick to check and easy to undo, plus one simple follow-up habit in the software you already use.

  • Point emergencies to the phone. The contact form lets people choose “Emergency Service.” A short line beside it could say: “For an emergency, please call (727) 761-7767 now.”
  • Ask where the job is. A city or ZIP box on the contact form, so no one has to call back just to check the area.
  • Keep track of every request. For each customer message: who has it, what happens next, and by when.

Please read these as questions, not corrections. You know your customers far better than I do.

We’d agree before starting: hours and pay, what I can access, who approves the work, and when we’d look at the results together.

What I’ve already built

An example dashboard for incoming messages

Calls, emails, texts, and website messages land in one place, and emergencies rise to the top. AI helpers sort the rest into electrical, office, and website lanes, each with a person in charge. They pull out the key details and draft a reply. A person approves every reply and handles every emergency.

Try it: adamjskoog.github.io/sspros-proposal/dashboard.html. Every message is made up, and nothing is sent.

How the AI did in testing

I tested the website-message sorting on 40 made-up messages I wrote. 39 of 40 passed the automatic checks, and all 6 emergencies went to a person. It still made mistakes: reading the answers by hand, I found two that stated things the customer never said.

A test I wrote myself doesn’t prove it works on real messages. Your team would check it on real work before relying on it.

Where AI could help next

  • Turning the estimator’s notes into a clean written proposal. A person sets every price.
  • Service reminders for generator and EV charger customers.
  • Turning a voice memo from the job site into a tidy job note.
  • Drafting project stories, social posts, and review requests from finished jobs.
  • A daily short list of public bids in your area. Worth talking about first.

Also built: a sample page for commercial work, 38 website ideas, and an office kit with a workbook and four message drafts.

A measured start

The first 90 days

Weeks 1–2: listen

Learn how a customer request moves through your office and which software you already use. Bring back a short list of suggestions for you to choose from.

By day 30: first results

One website fix you approved, live and checked. One simple follow-up habit in the system you already use, so every customer hears back.

By day 60: one AI task

Try the AI idea you picked on real work, with a person checking everything it produces. Keep track of the time it takes, including the checking.

Day 90: decide together

Keep it, change it, or stop it. Agree the role, hours, and pay, and whether to add marketing, hiring support, or public bids.

What we’d watch: how quickly customers hear back, whether requests arrive complete, that nothing is forgotten, and the time spent, including checking the AI’s work.
